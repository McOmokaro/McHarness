# The Segway Test

Three questions. An idea must pass **all three** to be worth building. Most ideas fail the second one, and almost all "trend" ideas fail the third.

Named after the Segway: a genuinely clever machine that answered the first question beautifully and the other two not at all. Everyone talked about it. Nobody needed it.

## The three questions

Ask them in order. Stop at the first "no".

### 1. Is it changing how work gets done?

Not *how people feel*, not *how they talk*. Work. A concrete job — a task that costs someone hours, dollars, or errors today — done materially differently because this exists.

- **Pass:** "It cuts invoice reconciliation from 6 hours/week to 20 minutes."
- **Fail:** "It's a beautiful interface for exploring ideas."

If it doesn't change a job, it's a hobby, a demo, or a feature — not a business.

### 2. Are businesses paying for it because it saves time or money?

The word that matters is **paying**, and the reason that matters is **time or money** — not novelty, not "it's AI", not curiosity.

- **Pass:** "Three clinics pay $400/mo because it removes a receptionist's 2 hrs/day of scheduling."
- **Fail:** "People say they'd love it." (Love is free; payment is the test.)
- **Fail:** "It's exciting/new/cool." (Excitement doesn't renew subscriptions.)

If no one is *already* paying, or demonstrably about to, you have a thesis, not a business. Be specific about who pays, how much, and what line-item it replaces or protects.

### 3. Would it still matter if nobody were talking about it?

Strip away the hype, the launch, the thread, the "everyone's doing X right now." If the idea is quiet and unglamorous and *still* solves a worsening, expensive problem — it survives.

- **Pass:** "It's boring bookkeeping automation. Nobody tweets about it. The pain is real and recurring."
- **Fail:** "It only makes sense because everyone is talking about [trend]." (You've built a bet on attention, not on value.)

Hype is a tailwind, never a foundation. A trend-following idea dies the day the trend does; a value-following idea compounds.

## How to run it

1. State the idea in one sentence: *who* pays *how much* to get *what job* done.
2. Answer Q1–Q3 with **evidence**, not assertion. Point to a real user, a real competitor making money, a real hours-saved number.
3. A "no" is a finding, not a failure — it means reshape the idea until all three answer yes, or drop it and keep looking. The cost of a false yes here is months of building the wrong thing.

## Failure modes this catches

- **The cool demo:** passes Q1 (impressive), fails Q2 (nobody pays).
- **The trend chase:** passes Q1/Q2 in the moment, fails Q3 (gone when the hype is).
- **The vitamin sold as a painkiller:** passes Q1 lightly, fails Q2 (nice, not necessary).
- **The "everyone needs this":** fails Q2 because "everyone" never signs the invoice — a specific somebody does.
